I tried the usual AI writing tools. They all have the same problem: a "tone" dropdown with options like Professional, Casual, Friendly, Authoritative. None of those sound like me. They sound like a content marketing template with the brand name swapped out.

## The idea

What if instead of picking a tone from a menu, you fed the model a corpus of things you actually wrote? Blog posts, commit messages, Slack rants, journal entries. Then asked it to draft something new in that voice.

That is Synvix. A local-first freewrite studio where the corpus is the style guide.

## How it works

You add your existing writing to a local corpus folder. When you start a freewrite session, Synvix pulls relevant samples from the corpus and gives them to Claude as context. The model drafts in your voice because it has your voice — not a description of your voice, but actual examples of it.

The freewrite flow is SSE-streamed. You type a rough idea, hit draft, and watch the model produce something that sounds like you wrote it on a good day. Then you revise, or start over, or keep the paragraph that landed and throw away the rest.

Drafts save as markdown files on disk. No database, no cloud sync, no export step. The file is the artifact.

## Why local-only

Synvix explicitly refuses to run on Vercel or any hosted platform. Your corpus is your writing — your voice, your ideas, your unfinished thoughts. That stays on your machine.

The technical stack reflects that choice: Next.js for the UI, Claude Code CLI for the LLM calls (stream-json mode), and Node 22's built-in SQLite for metadata. No external services. No API keys visible to a browser.

## What surprised me

The corpus approach works better than I expected for short-form writing. LinkedIn posts, tweet threads, README intros — the model picks up sentence length, paragraph rhythm, and the kinds of analogies you reach for. It does not pick up domain expertise, but it picks up cadence.

The other surprise was how much I use it for revision, not generation. I write a rough draft by hand, paste it in, and ask Synvix to tighten it in my voice. The output is usually closer to what I meant than what I typed.

## The wedge

Voice-from-samples is the wedge. Most AI writing tools optimize for volume. Synvix optimizes for sounding like the person who is using it. For a solo builder who publishes under their own name, that is the entire value proposition.
